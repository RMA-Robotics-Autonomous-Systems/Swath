          Sonar

          The water column under the fish returns nothing usable. It scales with how high the fish is flown, so it is set as a multiple of the altitude rather than as a fixed distance — one altitude is the usual figure.

          Spacing

          Three thresholds fall out of the geometry. Each one means something different on the seabed, so they are named for what they buy.

          Rig

          Waypoints steer the GPS antenna, and the fish is — astern of it. Every line is stretched by that much so the fish, not the boat, crosses the box.

          Both are legs of line either side of the box, drawn and steered as asked. The layback makes them cost different things.

          Run-in buys settling. A towed body needs about three cable lengths behind a turn — this rig is still out by 5.4 m at 25 m, 1.8 m at 100 m and 0.7 m past 400 m — and the fish gets the run-in plus the offset, because recording only starts once it has caught up to the edge: — of straight running.

          Run-out buys coverage, and that is why it has a floor the run-in has not: the fish is astern, so a leg that stops at the box edge leaves the fish short of it. Right now —.

          The turn allowance is dead time added to the estimate for each turn — slowing down, coming round, lining up and letting the fish settle. It changes the hours, never the geometry.

          GeoTIFF (.tif) comes in as a raster you can colour and shade; a single-band grid such as a multibeam DTM keeps its values, so the depth under the cursor is still a depth. GPX (.gpx) comes in as tracks, routes and waypoints.
